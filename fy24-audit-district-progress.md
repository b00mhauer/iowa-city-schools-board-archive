# What the district has done since the FY24 audit problems

*A companion note to my piece on the FY24 audit (released June 19, 2026).*

The FY24 audit covers **July 2023 through June 2024** — a window that is now
two years in the rearview mirror. Almost everything the auditors flagged
happened under a finance office that has since been substantially rebuilt, and
the district's own corrective-action plan and its most recent board updates
show real, documented movement. In fairness to where things stand today, here
is what Iowa City schools say they've already fixed and what's still in
progress — drawn from the board's own materials, primarily the **June 9, 2026**
board meeting packet (the most recent), and the district's written **Action
Plan in Response to Findings** that accompanies the audit.

## New financial leadership

The people who ran the books during the FY24 period are gone. The district
brought in an **interim CFO, Kim Michael-Lee, on Feb. 25, 2026** to stabilize
the office, and has since hired a **permanent CFO, Pat Moore** — announced
April 1 and active in the role since roughly May 18, 2026. Moore came from
Solon Community School District, where she had been CFO since 2004. The audit
catch-up plan and the monthly board reporting are now hers to run.

This sits inside a broader leadership turnover: Superintendent Matt Degner is
transitioning out of the superintendency, and the board has an **interim
superintendent search** underway, with a permanent search to follow.

## The books are largely caught up

The most concrete sign of progress is the reconciliation status chart CFO Moore
delivered to the board on June 4, 2026:

- **Bank reconciliations for 2023, 2024 and 2025 are complete** across the
  General, Schoolhouse, Activity, Insurance, Food Service and Payroll accounts.
- **FY26 reconciliations** were on track for completion by **mid-June 2026**.
- Starting **July 1, 2026**, the finance office moves to a **full monthly
  close and reconciliation process** — the "real-time, no lags" standard the
  audit response calls for, rather than the multi-year backlog that caused the
  problems in the first place.

## The overdue audits are being worked off in sequence

One of the audit's formal findings was that the FY23 and FY24 single audits
weren't filed on time. The district now has a paid, multi-year engagement with
**Bohnsack & Frommelt** and a clear timeline:

- **FY24:** fieldwork finished **April 17, 2026**; preliminary report delivered
  on site May 16; the district was working to present the **final report to the
  board in June 2026** (targeting the June 23 meeting).
- **FY25:** fieldwork begins August 2026, with completion expected by late
  October and the report to the board in **November 2026**.
- **FY26:** on track to be filed by the **March 31, 2027 statutory deadline** —
  i.e., current.
- A **FY23 restatement** is also in progress.

## Board-level oversight changes since January 2026

After learning of the financial situation in January 2026, the board took a
series of governance actions (per its "Board Financial Leadership Update"):

- Opened a **direct line to district legal counsel** and to the audit firm.
- **Notified the State Auditor** and passed a resolution requesting a
  **reaudit** under Iowa Code 11.6(4) once the FY24/FY25 audits are done.
- Required a **monthly written report** of all receipts, disbursements and fund
  balances, reported directly to the board.
- Began **re-chartering its Financial Oversight Committee** to add community
  financial expertise, and arranged **school-finance training** for board
  members through the Iowa Association of School Boards.
- Tightened **P-Card policy and monitoring**, adding monthly p-card usage to
  the consent agenda.
- Created an **anonymous staff feedback channel**, and committed to hiring an
  independent firm for a **forensic audit** after the FY24–FY25 audits close.

The board also adopted a multi-stage stability plan: spring 2026 budget
reductions, a summer 2026 organizational/staffing review, and a 2026–27
attendance-center capacity and class-size study.

## Where specific findings stand

The district's action plan addresses each finding directly. A few highlights:

- **Segregation of duties (payroll, disbursements, receipts):** payroll duties
  now split across three staff with controller approval; multi-step requisition
  approval; staff training documents in development.
- **Activity Fund deficit:** COO Curt Pratt and the CFO presented the activity
  fund to the board in June 2026 and met with athletic directors and principals
  on **June 17, 2026**, with coding-and-procedure professional development set
  for mid-July.
- **Statutory filings (Form 509A):** the **2024 form has been filed** (late
  penalties waived) and the **2025 form** submitted to the actuary.
- **Federal capital assets:** the district is going live with a **fixed-asset
  module** in its finance software in FY27 for proper tracking and depreciation.

## The honest caveat

Plenty of this is still "in progress" rather than "done" — FY26 reconciliations,
the FY25 and FY26 audits, the forensic audit, the new oversight committee, and a
permanent superintendent are all ahead, not behind. But the gap between the
period the audit examined and where the district stands in mid-2026 is real: new
finance leadership, three years of reconciliations completed, the FY24 audit
finally at the finish line, and a board that has put monthly reporting and
outside scrutiny in place.
